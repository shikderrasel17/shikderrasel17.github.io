Rasel Shikder

I work with data and digital systems for organizations, and I research the same things academically.

Right now: Senior Executive, Sales-MIS at Bashundhara Group in Dhaka. I build the reports and dashboards that help a company operating across the country make decisions.

I was born and raised in Bangladesh, and I studied Management Information Systems at the University of Dhaka, a BBA first, then an MBA in the same subject. I did not pick MIS because I had a grand plan. I picked it because it sat between two things I liked: business, which I understood from watching people around me run small operations, and computers, which I had been curious about since I was a kid. It turned out to be a good match, and most of what I do today still traces back to that overlap.

Right now I work as a Senior Executive in Sales-MIS at Bashundhara Group, one of the larger business groups in Bangladesh, with operations across the whole country. My actual day looks less exciting than the job title. It is SAP entries, sales numbers, price approvals, and reports that go up the chain so people above me can make calls with real money attached. Before this I spent close to a year at Quasem Foundation, where I helped run the reporting side of a network of eye hospitals and vision centers spread across the country. That job taught me more than any course did about what happens when the people using a system are not technical, and the data is never as clean as you would want.

What I am actually good at is turning that kind of mess into something usable. I build reports and dashboards in Power BI and Excel, I write small apps and automations with Google Apps Script and Python, and I set up data pipelines so information moves cleanly between departments instead of sitting stuck in someone's inbox. It is not glamorous work, but it is the kind of work that a large organization genuinely runs on.

After office hours I do research on the same problems, from a more formal angle. My areas are how organizations adopt digital systems, how those systems support decisions in health settings, and more broadly how people behave inside large digital systems once they are actually rolled out. I am not far along yet, a couple of papers in progress and one published a few years back on a different topic, but I am including the real status next to each one because I would rather you see where I actually am than a version that sounds more finished than it is.

Outside of work, a few things have stuck with me for years. I played football through university and ended up captaining my department's team for a season, not because I was the most talented player, but because someone had to hold the team together, and I did not mind doing it. We won a few things together, which still means more to me than it probably should. I also tend to end up organizing things: a 600-student olympiad for kids from rural schools, a flood relief collection when the floods hit Bangladesh hard, a couple of alumni tournaments. None of it was planned. Someone usually just needed to step up, and I was around.

I got married in the middle of all this, and I am currently working through a CMA qualification alongside everything else, two levels down, three to go, whenever I find the time. I am also mid-way through applying to PhD programs in Information Systems, after an earlier funded offer from a US university got stuck at the visa stage. That part of the story is not finished yet, so I will not pretend it is.

If you are hiring and want the professional side, the experience page has my job resume and what I have actually built at each place. Or just write to me, I answer most messages within a day or two.
