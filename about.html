About

The short version of who's behind the CV.

I grew up curious about why systems work the way they do, and that habit never really left. These days I run sales-MIS operations at Bashundhara Group, one of Bangladesh's largest conglomerates, and in the evenings I'm just as likely to be cleaning a messy dataset for a research paper as I am tuning a report for tomorrow's meeting.

My background is Management Information Systems — a BBA and an MBA, both from the University of Dhaka. Along the way I've built ERP and hospital-information-system reporting for a thirteen-center eye-care network, coordinated donor reports for international NGOs, and spent more evenings than I'd like to admit chasing down a mismatched SAP transaction before a deadline.

Lately most of my attention goes to explainable machine learning: models that don't just predict who's at risk of dropping out of school, or falling through the cracks of a health system, but explain why clearly enough that someone without a technical background can actually act on it. That question sits right between my day job and my research, which is exactly where I like to work.

The interesting problems are rarely about the model. They're about whether the person reading the output can trust it enough to act.

Outside work, I captained my university's football team for a season, which taught me more about handling pressure than most textbooks did. I also tend to organize things when nobody else will — a 600-participant olympiad for rural students, a flood-relief drive, an alumni tournament. If there's a gap that needs filling, I usually end up the one filling it.

If you want the specifics, the research and industry work pages cover them in detail, and the CV has the full record. This page is just the short version.
