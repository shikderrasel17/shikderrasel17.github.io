About

The long version. No résumé polish, just how things actually happened.

I was born and raised in Bangladesh, and I studied Management Information Systems at the University of Dhaka — a BBA first, then an MBA in the same subject. I didn't pick MIS because I had a grand plan. I picked it because it sat between two things I liked: business, which I understood from watching people around me run small operations, and computers, which I'd been curious about since I was a kid. It turned out to be a good match, and most of what I do today still traces back to that overlap.

Right now I work as a Senior Executive in Sales-MIS at Bashundhara Group, one of the larger business groups in Bangladesh. My actual day looks less exciting than the job title: SAP entries, sales numbers, price approvals, and reports that go up the chain so people above me can make calls with real money attached. Before this I spent close to a year at Quasem Foundation, where I helped run the reporting side of a network of eye hospitals and vision centers spread across the country. That job taught me more than any course did about what happens when the people using a system aren't technical, and the data is never as clean as you'd want.

I'm also trying, slowly, to build a research side. Right now that means explainable machine learning: models that don't just spit out a prediction, but can explain themselves well enough that a teacher or a health worker who has never touched Python can still trust the answer. I'm not there yet in any big way — a couple of papers in progress, one published a few years back on a completely different topic. I'm including all of it here honestly, with the actual status next to each one, because I'd rather you see where I really am than a version that sounds more finished than it is.

I think the hardest part of any model isn't getting it accurate. It's getting someone who doesn't trust computers to believe the answer enough to act on it.

Outside of work, a few things have stuck with me for years. I played football through university and ended up captaining my department's team for a season — not because I was the most talented player, but because someone had to hold the team together, and I didn't mind doing it. We won a few things together, which still means more to me than it probably should. I also tend to end up organizing things: a 600-student olympiad for kids from rural schools, a flood relief collection when the floods hit Bangladesh hard, a couple of alumni tournaments. None of it was planned. Someone usually just needed to step up, and I was around.

I got married in the middle of all this, and I'm currently working through a CMA qualification alongside everything else — two levels down, three to go, whenever I find the time. I'm also mid-way through applying to PhD programs in Information Systems, after an earlier funded offer from a US university got stuck at the visa stage. That part of the story isn't finished yet, so I won't pretend it is.

If you want the details instead of the summary: research and industry work go deeper on what I actually do, and the CV has the full record, dates included. Or just write to me — I answer most messages within a day or two.
