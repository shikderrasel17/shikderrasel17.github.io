Leadership & Community

Most of this happened alongside work or study, not instead of it. Someone usually just needed to step up, and I was around. Over time it added up to a real pattern, not a one-off line on a resume.

sports_soccer Football, and managing a team

I played football through university and took on managing my department's team for a season, sorting out who plays where, keeping practice on schedule, and holding the group together through a rough stretch of results. I was not the most talented player on the squad. Someone had to handle the organizing side, and I did not mind doing it. We won the MIS-IUG championship together, along with the Tripod & Bros tournament, and those wins still mean more to me than they probably should.

Team leadership Conflict resolution Consistency under pressure

groups Organizing a 600-student olympiad

I helped organize an olympiad for roughly 600 students from rural schools, the kind of event where the actual work is almost entirely logistics: transport, registration, volunteers, prizes, and a schedule that has to survive contact with several hundred children at once. Nothing about it was planned by me from the start. I stepped in because the team needed someone to own the coordination, and it turned into one of the more satisfying things I have helped run.

Event coordination Volunteer management Logistics under pressure

volunteer_activism Flood relief, Dhaka

When the floods hit Bangladesh hard, I helped organize a relief collection and distribution effort in Dhaka, gathering supplies and getting them into the hands of people who needed them, not sitting in a warehouse. It is unglamorous work, mostly carrying, sorting, and making sure the right household gets the right package. I keep coming back to this kind of work because the need is direct and the results are visible the same day.

Crisis response Resource coordination Community organizing

workspace_premium Case competitions and public speaking

Outside of football and community work, I competed in a run of business case competitions, Battle of Minds among them, and spoke at events including the BIZnnovation grand finale and a couple of alumni gatherings. These were not glamorous wins either, most of the value was in learning to build an argument quickly under a deadline and then stand in front of a room and defend it.
