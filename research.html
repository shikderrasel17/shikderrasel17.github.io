Research

What I am actually asking

I did not come to research from a pure academic background. Before I thought seriously about a PhD, I was coordinating data and reporting systems for a thirteen-center eye-care network, and I now run sales and executive reporting for a large industrial group that operates across the whole country. Both jobs put me in the same position over and over: a system gets introduced, and then someone has to figure out why it is not being used the way it was designed to be used, or why the people meant to benefit from it are not getting anything out of it.

I want to be specific about where I sit in this field, because it matters for the kind of PhD I am looking for. I am not trying to build a new model or design a new system as an end in itself, that is design science, and it is not where my interest is. I am more interested in the behavioral side: how a system that already exists, or an idea that is already out there, actually changes a social situation once real people have to live with it. What changes in how people think about a problem, how they make decisions, how a community or an organization behaves, once a new tool or idea is placed in front of them. The people side, not the building side. If you work in behavioral IS research and this sounds like the same question you are asking, I would like to hear from you.

That is the question underneath everything I research. Organizations, especially in developing countries, adopt digital systems for good reasons, an ERP, a hospital information system, a reporting dashboard, but the gap between buying the system and actually getting value out of it is where most of the real problems live. I want to understand that gap, and I want to understand it in settings that look like the ones I have actually worked in, not just the well-resourced ones that most of the existing research is built on.

My research interest and my professional work are not two separate things I am trying to balance. I am doing, day to day, exactly what I want to study more formally later, and my academic background is in exactly the same area. That overlap is the main reason I am pursuing a PhD in the first place, not a side note.

My three research areas

IS and AI enabled Digital Transformation

Why organizations, particularly in developing countries, struggle to get real value out of digital systems once they are in place, and where AI can genuinely close that gap versus where it just adds another layer of complexity.

e-Health Decision-making

Drawing directly on my time coordinating a Hospital Information System across a multi-center eye-care network. Which ERP and HIS design choices actually speed up good clinical and operational decisions, and where an analytics layer helps versus where it just adds noise.

Computational Social Science

Using data and computational methods to study how people actually behave inside large systems, education, health, and organizations, rather than how they are assumed to behave on paper.
