Research

Explainable machine learning for decisions in education and health, in resource-constrained settings.

Research statement

My research asks how machine learning models can support high-stakes decisions in organizations that operate with limited resources and limited technical capacity, without becoming black boxes those organizations cannot trust or govern. I focus on two settings: predicting student dropout risk in higher education, and analyzing e-health decision-making, in both cases using explainable ML methods so that the reasoning behind a prediction is visible to the people who have to act on it, not only its output.

Before starting doctoral study I coordinated data and reporting systems for a 13-center eye-care network and now run sales and executive MIS for a large industrial group in Bangladesh. Both roles gave me a close view of what happens when analytics are built for a resource-constrained organization rather than for a data-rich one: incomplete records, small sample sizes, staff with no data science background, and decisions that carry real consequences for real people. That is the setting my research is built for.

Research themes

Explainable ML for student dropout prediction

Can interpretable model architectures identify at-risk students early enough, and clearly enough, for advisors without a technical background to act on the prediction? Focused on fairness across demographic subgroups, not only aggregate accuracy.

Fairness in South Asian e-learning analytics

How do dropout and performance models trained on South Asian e-learning data behave across gender, region, and socioeconomic subgroups, and what does that imply for deploying them in real institutions?

Information systems for e-health decision-making

Drawing on direct experience coordinating a Hospital Information System across a multi-center eye-care network: how ERP/HIS design choices shape the quality and speed of clinical and operational decisions, and what an analytics layer on top of that system should and should not automate.

Methods toolkit

Python (Pandas, scikit-learn, TensorFlow, Keras) Explainable ML (SHAP-style interpretation) Statistical testing (Stata, SPSS) Survey and institutional data analysis SQL Power BI / Tableau
