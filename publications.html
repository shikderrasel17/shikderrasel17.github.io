Submitted to LAK 2027

Disability Raises a Poor Student's Risk of Dropping Out Far More than a Rich One's, but Does the Early-Warning System Still Underestimate It? A Landmark Audit Using OULAD

International Conference on Learning Analytics and Knowledge (LAK 2027), short paper

The question: Universities use early-warning systems to flag students who are at risk of dropping out. This paper audits one of them to see whether it treats all students fairly.

The data: A public dataset of more than 31,000 course enrolments from a UK open university.

What we found: Disabled students from poorer neighbourhoods are flagged on time, but the system still underestimates how likely they are to leave. A tool can look fair on paper and still let down the students who need it most.


Submitted to ICCIT 2026

Examining Whether Digital Financial Inclusion Reduces Household Financial Vulnerability to Health Shocks in South Asia Using Explainable Machine Learning

International Conference on Computer and Information Technology (ICCIT 2026), IEEE

The question: Governments have promoted bank accounts and mobile money as protection against financial shocks. This paper tests whether having an account keeps a family from borrowing when someone falls ill.

The data: World Bank survey data from five South Asian countries.

What we found: No clear sign that having an account does. How a household already handles money day to day mattered much more.
